docs/governance.md                # Phoenix Print Farm — Governance Framework (2026)

This document defines the governance framework for Phoenix Print Farms. It establishes
how authority, decision-making, oversight, and compliance are structured to preserve
sovereignty, accountability, and operational integrity.

Governance is designed to prevent capture by vendors, operators, or external platforms
while enabling scalable, repeatable deployment across jurisdictions.

---

## 1. Governance Objectives

Phoenix Print Farm governance exists to:

- Maintain sovereign control over manufacturing systems
- Separate authority from day-to-day operations
- Prevent unauthorized changes to production capability
- Ensure compliance without operational slowdown
- Enable replication across regions and nations

Governance is structural, not political.

---

## 2. Authority Structure

Phoenix governance operates across three layers of authority.

### 2.1 Sovereign Authority
- Holds ultimate jurisdiction over deployment and operation
- Defines national or regional manufacturing policy
- Approves cross-border execution or export rules
- Cannot be overridden by vendors or operators

### 2.2 System Authority
- Controls system configuration and evolution
- Approves new materials, machine classes, and capabilities
- Maintains architectural integrity
- Oversees compliance enforcement mechanisms

### 2.3 Operational Authority
- Executes approved production
- Maintains uptime and reliability
- Operates strictly within defined boundaries
- Has no authority to expand scope unilaterally

Authority is explicit and non-overlapping.

---

## 3. Decision Rights

Decisions are classified to prevent ambiguity.

### 3.1 Strategic Decisions
Examples:
- Deployment locations
- Jurisdictional scope
- Expansion of manufacturing classes

Approval: Sovereign Authority

### 3.2 System Decisions
Examples:
- Software updates
- Material certification
- Security policy changes

Approval: System Authority

### 3.3 Operational Decisions
Examples:
- Job scheduling
- Maintenance execution
- Energy mode selection

Approval: Operational Authority

Unauthorized decisions are treated as governance violations.

---

## 4. Change Management

Phoenix Print Farms do not evolve informally.

All changes follow a controlled process:

1. Proposal submission
2. Risk and impact review
3. Authority approval
4. Logged deployment
5. Post-change validation

Emergency changes are permitted only under documented exception protocols.

---

## 5. Jurisdiction & Sovereignty

Each Phoenix Print Farm operates under a defined jurisdiction.

Governance enforces:
- Local control of execution rights
- Local retention of audit logs
- Jurisdictional boundaries on file execution
- No external veto over production

No foreign cloud, vendor, or platform may unilaterally restrict operation.

---

## 6. Compliance Model

Compliance is continuous, not periodic.

Governance ensures:
- Immutable production logs
- Material traceability
- Operator accountability
- Audit-ready state at all times

Compliance mechanisms are embedded in system design,
not layered on afterward.

---

## 7. Vendor & Partner Governance

Vendors and partners:

- Have no governance authority
- Cannot access production decisions
- Cannot alter system rules
- Are replaceable without system redesign

Phoenix governance is vendor-neutral by design.

---

## 8. Conflict Resolution

Governance conflicts are resolved by:

1. Reference to documented authority boundaries
2. Review of system logs and approvals
3. Escalation to Sovereign Authority if required

No informal overrides are permitted.

---

## 9. Replication & Standardization

This governance framework is designed to be:

- Reproducible across jurisdictions
- Adaptable to local law without system redesign
- Consistent across all Phoenix deployments

Standardization ensures interoperability without loss of sovereignty.

---

## 10. Summary

The Phoenix Print Farm governance framework:

- Separates power from execution
- Prevents unauthorized system drift
- Preserves national manufacturing control
- Enables scalable, compliant deployment
- Eliminates dependence on external platforms

Governance is not overhead.
It is the mechanism that makes sovereign manufacturing possible.
